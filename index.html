The Physics Explained

1. Would Gravity Stop Me? How Far Could I Make It?

Gravity pulls toward the anchor; available grip and your speed determine how you move.

Earth is a sphere ($R_E \approx 6,371\text{ km}$). Earth's gravity always points towards its center of mass. At the plank's anchor ($x = 0$), gravity points straight down into the plank ($9.81\text{ m/s}^2$).

As you run forward along the laser-straight plank, Earth curves away beneath you. This tilts the gravitational pull vector backward toward the center point by an angle:

θ = arctan(x / R_E)
  • 336 km out (Alt 8.85 km): You reach the altitude of Mt. Everest. The slope is 3.0°. Air pressure drops below 35 kPa; you run out of oxygen and suffocate without a breathing apparatus.
  • 1,133 km out (Alt 100 km): You cross the Kármán line into space vacuum on an 18% uphill road grade (10.1° slope).
  • 3,678 km out (Alt 985 km): The slope reaches 30.0°! The backward pull ($3.68\text{ m/s}^2$) exceeds the maximum friction of rubber shoes ($F_{friction} = \mu F_N$). Your feet slip out and you slide backwards towards Earth!
  • 6,371 km out (Alt 2,639 km): You are 1 Earth radius out. The plank is a 45° vertical cliff climbing into deep space!

2. Would the Plank Look Bent From My Perspective?

It depends on your frame of reference!

  • Gravity-Aligned View (Human Inner Ear): Humans balance so that our feet point along local gravity. If your camera aligns with gravity, the flat plank visually curves UPWARD into the sky like a colossal ramp or loop-de-loop! Even though the plank is physically straight, you feel like you are standing in the bottom of a giant bowl.
  • Plank-Aligned View: If you force your camera to stay flush with the plank deck, the plank looks straight ahead, but looking over the side, Earth curves down, drops into a bottomless abyss, and tilts backwards beneath you!

Use the [1. Gravity-Aligned] and [2. Plank-Aligned] buttons in the bottom dock to toggle between these two perspectives in real time!

Movement and temperature model

W/S move along the plank; A/D change lanes. Looking around does not steer you. Auto-run keeps the last W/S direction. Waypoint jumps preserve your lane.

Walk and Run use gravity, limited shoe traction, sliding friction, braking and air resistance. Mag-boots add grip. The high-speed presets use a powered travel assist; they are not human running.

Air temperature and density use a layered standard atmosphere, not local weather. Above the model range no air temperature is invented. Suit heating, oxygen supply, wind and structural bending are not simulated.
